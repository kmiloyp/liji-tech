**Short answer:** UN3480 is for lithium ion batteries shipped on their own. UN3481 is for lithium ion batteries contained in equipment, or packed with it. UN3090 and UN3091 cover the same two cases for lithium metal batteries.

## The four marks side by side

| Mark | Battery type | How it ships |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UN3480 | Lithium ion | On its own |
| UN3481 | Lithium ion | Contained in equipment, or packed with it |
| UN3090 | Lithium metal | On its own |
| UN3091 | Lithium metal | Contained in equipment, or packed with it |

## Two questions decide the mark

**1. Is the battery lithium ion or lithium metal?** Lithium ion batteries are the rechargeable kind. Lithium metal batteries are a different chemistry. The first part of the choice is UN3480/UN3481 for lithium ion, or UN3090/UN3091 for lithium metal.

**2. Does the battery travel alone, or with equipment?** "On its own" means a box of batteries. "Contained in equipment" means the battery is inside the device, like a battery inside a phone. "Packed with equipment" means the battery and the device travel in the same box, but the battery is not installed.

## What about the Class 9 label?

Lithium batteries belong to Class 9, miscellaneous dangerous goods. Many shipments carry the Class 9 lithium battery hazard label together with the lithium battery mark. We print that label at 4 × 4 in.

> Reference only. The shipper classifies the goods and chooses the marks under the current IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, the IMDG Code and 49 CFR.
