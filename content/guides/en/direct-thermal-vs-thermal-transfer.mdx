**Short answer:** Direct thermal labels print with heat alone, so there is no ribbon. Thermal transfer labels print with a ribbon, last longer and resist rubbing.

## How each one prints

### Direct thermal

The printer heats the label itself, so there is no ribbon. It suits labels with a short life, such as shipping labels. Heat and sunlight fade the print.

### Thermal transfer

The printer melts ink from a ribbon onto the label. The print lasts longer and resists rubbing, which suits inventory and product labels.

## Side by side

| | Direct thermal | Thermal transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ribbon | Not needed | Needed |
| Print life | Short: heat and sunlight fade it | Longer, resists rubbing |
| Good for | Shipping labels | Inventory and product labels |

## How to choose

- The label only has to last the trip: **direct thermal** is the simpler option.
- The label has to stay readable on a shelf, in storage or on a product: **thermal transfer**.
